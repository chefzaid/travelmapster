    How the 90/180 rule works

    • 90 days in any 180. Visitors on a short stay may spend at most 90 days in the Schengen area within any 180-day period, with or without a visa.
    • The window rolls. On any day, look back over that day and the 179 days before it. The days you spent in the area in that window must not add up to more than 90.
    • Entry and exit days count. Any part of a day in the area counts as a whole day, so a day trip counts as one.
    • One area, one count. Days in every member country add up together; moving from France to Spain does not reset anything.
    • Days come back slowly. A day drops out of the count 180 days after it, so after a long stay you regain days one by one.

    Days spent under a residence permit or a long-stay national visa of a member country do not count toward the 90. This calculator is a guide, not legal advice: border officers make the final decision.

    Which countries count?

    The Schengen area has 29 members as of 2026, including Bulgaria and Romania since 1 January 2025:

    • Austria
    • Belgium
    • Bulgaria
    • Croatia
    • Czechia
    • Denmark
    • Estonia
    • Finland
    • France
    • Germany
    • Greece
    • Hungary
    • Iceland
    • Italy
    • Latvia
    • Liechtenstein
    • Lithuania
    • Luxembourg
    • Malta
    • Netherlands
    • Norway
    • Poland
    • Portugal
    • Romania
    • Slovakia
    • Slovenia
    • Spain
    • Sweden
    • Switzerland

    Days in Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City count too, as they are reached only through the area. Cyprus and Ireland (EU members outside the area) and Andorra do not count, and neither do overseas territories outside Europe, such as French Guiana or Réunion.
